# Salt and Iron

*Nobody reads this but its owner.*

---

Four hundred marks. Eleven marks of fee. I have done the sums in the
dark like a fool and the sums are the same sums every time.

She said *lamp oil*. Lamp oil in a brass-collared jar does not keep a
tide in it, and I have carried oil for six years, and oil does not keep
time.

And she looked at me when she said it. Not at Orson. At me. The way you
look at a person you are about to ask something you cannot take back.

There is a thing I have never said to anyone and I am going to write it
here, because the jar is on the table and I cannot sleep.

She holds her hands the way my mother held them. Both hands, turned over
at the wrist, so that you can see the palms — my mother did that, and
Hessa Cray does that, and my mother has been dead nine years and I was
seven.

I am not going to ask. If I ask, she will tell me the truth, and the truth
will be a thing I have to carry, and I have four hundred marks to carry
already.

The tide comes in at the spring. Bel said the jar keeps the hour. So
somewhere in that jar there is the hour, and if I open it I will know
exactly how long I have.

---

*I will open it tonight. Whatever else happens, I will know the hour.*
