# Pay the Tide

**The debt.** Four hundred marks, owed to [[Hessa Cray]] for the
Brinebacks.

**How it happened.** On the night of the wreck the party took a job from
Hessa Cray's runner: carry one sealed crate of lamp oil from the tide
landing to the Tide House on Sallow Lane, do not stop, do not ask what is
in it. The crate was salt. They were in the fog on the Causeway and took
the channel that goes south when the channel that goes north was the
right one, and the lighter put a shoulder. The crate went into the water.

Salt that spills is salt that is found. The Crown's reeve found it. Hessa
Cray paid the reeve four hundred marks to keep it quiet, and then she
wrote four hundred marks against the party.

**What is owed.** Four hundred salt-marks. The party have about sixty.

**When it is due.** The spring tide. Not a date — a tide. It falls in
three weeks, and the ledger does not care whether the party are ready.

**What happens if it is not paid.** The name goes on the Salt Roll at the
head of the Causeway, in chalk, read aloud at every tide. Not a beating.
Worse: a Saltmere where everyone knows, and the party are still here.

**What the party know.** That they owe it, that Hessa Cray holds the
bond, and that the Brinebacks' boats work at night. They do not know that
the salt was meant to go into the water.

## The shape of it

Three ways this ends, and the party can walk into all three:

- **Pay it.** Sixty marks now and three hundred and forty later, out of
  work nobody has found yet.
- **Refuse it.** The Roll, the Causeway, and a city that reads names
  aloud.
- **Change what it is.** [[The Tideglass]] is worth more than four hundred
  marks to the Brinebacks, and it is in the party's hands. Offer it back
  and the debt does not go away — it becomes something else, and what it
  becomes is Hessa Cray's to decide.
