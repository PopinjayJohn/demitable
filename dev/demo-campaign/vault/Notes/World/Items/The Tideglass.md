# The Tideglass

A brass-and-glass instrument in a jar of brine, about the size of a
frying pan and heavier than it looks. The Brinebacks keep it in the
counting-house and use it to say what hour the tide will turn — not what
the tide will do, but the hour, to the quarter, which is the only part of
the sea that is worth money.

It came out of the *Gannet* with the rest of the wreck, and it is the one
thing in that water nobody can explain. The brine inside the jar is not
seawater. The glass does not fog in the damp. And the tide inside it
keeps its own time, which is either a piece of excellent glassmaking or
something else entirely.

The party has it. That is the entire problem. A tide-predictor in the
right hands makes the spring tide the most precise moment in Saltmere,
and the Brinebacks' ledger comes due at the spring tide.
