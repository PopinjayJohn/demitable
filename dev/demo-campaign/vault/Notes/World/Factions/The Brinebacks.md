# The Brinebacks

A crew, not a company. Eleven regulars, four boats, and a counting-house
at the Tide House on Sallow Lane that is legally a chandlery and
unofficially a bank.

They run untaxed salt out of the marsh and up the creeks to the coast
road. They also carry other people's untaxed salt, for a fee, because a
man who carries salt for money is a transporter and a man who carries
salt for his own profit is a smuggler, and only one of those is a word
that appears in a sentence in front of the Crown's reeve.

They have never broken the law in a way anyone could write down. The
reeve takes a cut and calls it a licence.

**The ledger.** The Brinebacks do not record debts in coin. They record
them in tides: *owed at the spring tide*, *owed at the first dark of the
new moon*, *owed when the water turns*. A tide comes due at an hour
nobody can argue with, and a debtor who does not pay is named on the
**Salt Roll**, which is chalked on the tide wall at the head of the
Causeway and read aloud at every tide by whoever is paid to read it.
Names come off the Roll only when the marks are paid. The Roll is read
by the whole city, every day, for as long as it takes.

**Colours.** Salt-white and tar-black. The sign is a gull with its head
turned inland.

**Who runs them:** [[Hessa Cray]], who has been the Brinebacks' factor
for eleven years, and who does not own the crew and does not act like
somebody who does.
