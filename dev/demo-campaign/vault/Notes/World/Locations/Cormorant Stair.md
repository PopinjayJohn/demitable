# Cormorant Stair

The old smuggler's cut. Four hundred steps down from the salt-pan dyke to
a stone landing that the sea covers at every tide above the first hour
after midnight. The steps are cut into the marsh wall and they are older
than the Causeway, and the Causeway was built over the top of them
because the Crown preferred a bridge to a staircase nobody would admit
to owning.

The cut runs east under the Causeway from the landing, and the water in
it moves faster than the water anywhere else in Saltmere, and the walls
are wet the whole day. Below the landing the cut widens into a basin, and
there is a stone sill at the basin mouth that the *Gannet* could cross
and nothing wider could.

> [!warning] The second hour
> After the second hour of the flood the cut runs too fast to row. Time
> the descent for slack water, or do not come back up.

```ad-tip
title: Running the Stair
The cut is a set piece, not a corridor: read the water first, the walls
second, and the basin last. If the party rushes it, the tide does the
describing.
```

> [!secret] The room at the bottom
> Under the basin there is a room, and the room is dry, and the room is
> where the Brinebacks keep the ledger — the real one, in ink, with the
> salt-names and the tide-hours and the interest written out in a hand
> nobody in the crew can read. The Stair is not a smuggling cut. It is
> where the debt is stored. ^ledgerroom
